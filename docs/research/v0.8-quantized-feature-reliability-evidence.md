# v0.8 quantized-feature reliability evidence

## Result

Dequantized magnitude features preserve high-precision tensor rankings across
Q4_K, Q5_K, Q6_K, and Q8_0, but not every feature is equally reliable. Tensor RMS
rank correlation is 0.9994 for Q4_K and 1.0 for the other codecs. Signed weight
means have 3.1%–28.8% mean relative error because their references are often near
zero, and quantized sparsity is not a faithful proxy for high-precision sparsity.

| Codec | Weight RMSE | RMS relative error | RMS rank ρ | Abs-mean relative error | Mean relative error | Sparsity absolute error |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Q4_K | 0.010553 | 0.3543% | 0.9994 | 0.4021% | 28.80% | 0.020 pp |
| Q5_K | 0.005144 | 0.0661% | 1.0000 | 0.0933% | 24.94% | 0.005 pp |
| Q6_K | 0.002429 | 0.0187% | 1.0000 | 0.0427% | 5.39% | 2.897 pp |
| Q8_0 | 0.000743 | 0.0077% | 1.0000 | 0.0088% | 3.14% | 0.845 pp |

Q6_K's zero-level representation creates the largest sparsity distortion despite
its otherwise low magnitude-feature error. Q8_0 has the lowest overall numeric
error. These results support locally dequantized RMS, variance, and absolute-mean
features for ranking, but they do not support treating decoded zero counts or tiny
signed means as stable high-precision features without an error-aware policy.

## Direct encoded features and resource cost

The v1 direct extractor safely exposes encoded geometry and primary-scale summaries;
decoded semantic moments continue to return `local_decode_required`. Primary scale
is compared to high-precision tensor RMS after z-score normalization because scale
units differ by codec.

| Codec | Direct-scale rank ρ vs RMS | Direct time | Decode + features | Direct speedup | Encoded bytes (27 samples) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Q4_K | 0.8394 | 0.581 ms | 43.533 ms | 74.9× | 62,208 |
| Q5_K | 0.8394 | 0.378 ms | 31.719 ms | 83.9× | 76,032 |
| Q6_K | 0.8120 | 0.529 ms | 35.764 ms | 67.6× | 90,720 |
| Q8_0 | 0.9573 | 1.742 ms | 23.393 ms | 13.4× | 117,504 |

Direct scale is a useful cheap coarse signal, particularly for Q8_0, but its
0.81–0.96 rank correlation is materially weaker than locally dequantized RMS. The
direct path remains intentionally fail-closed for weight mean, variance, and
sparsity rather than inventing unsafe encoded approximations.

Pure-Python encode time was 50.0 ms (Q4_K), 36.5 ms (Q5_K), 949.1 ms (Q6_K), and
23.4 ms (Q8_0). These timings characterize the current reference codecs on this PC,
not optimized llama.cpp throughput; Q6_K encode is the clear implementation hotspot.

## Protocol and provenance update

- The study samples 4,096 evenly spaced float32-promoted values from gate, up, and
  down projections at the first, middle, and last layer of SmolLM2-135M,
  SmolLM2-360M, and Qwen2.5-0.5B: 27 tensors and 110,592 values total.
- Every sample is matched across all four codecs. Each payload is encoded by the
  ModelSurgeon codec, read by the direct extractor, locally decoded, and compared
  with the same high-precision values.
- Per-feature records include signed bias, error variance, mean and maximum absolute
  error, mean relative absolute error, sample count, and Spearman rank correlation.
- `EmpiricalQuantizedFeatureErrorModel` now validates those persisted records and
  converts them into `PrecisionProvenance`/`ErrorProvenance` for locally dequantized
  features. The provenance method includes the exact study SHA-256, codec, v1 codec
  version, reference dtype, absolute error, and relative error.
- Sample manifests retain immutable model revisions, tensor names, and SHA-256
  digests of the exact sampled float64 sequences. Raw weights are not duplicated in
  Git.

## Validity boundary

The deterministic samples cover 27 MLP tensors, not every tensor or full weight
distribution. Encoding/decoding uses the project's Python reference codecs rather
than optimized llama.cpp kernels. Direct primary scale is a codec-specific surrogate
whose normalized comparison cannot be interpreted as raw-value bias. Error models
are empirical for this model/tensor sample and should be widened or replaced when
larger-model evidence becomes available. Activation and gradient quantization are
outside this weight-feature study.
