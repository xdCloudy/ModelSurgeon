# v0.8 Q8 iterative versus one-shot pruning evidence

## Result

At a matched final budget of 10 simultaneously masked MLP channels, iterative
evaluate/retrain produced a slightly better cumulative quality result than one-shot
ranking, but cost substantially more and did not improve latency. The two policies
selected nine channels in common.

| Arm | Final ΔPPL | Baseline / masked median forward | Latency delta | Total study time | Measured GPU hours | Quantization error |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| one-shot | -0.19473 | 24.347 / 24.629 ms | +0.283 ms | 1.536 s | 0.000339 | 0 |
| iterative | -0.20598 | 23.584 / 24.016 ms | +0.432 ms | 2.703 s | 0.000479 | 0 |

Both combined masks improved perplexity on this short calibration sample, with the
iterative set improving it by another 0.01126. Iterative selection required 10
LightGBM fits and the revelation of 10 measured single-channel outcomes; one-shot
required one fit and no intermediate label revelation. Its total study time was
1.76× one-shot and its accounted GPU time was 1.42× one-shot.

Neither arm delivered a latency gain. These are reversible functional masks, so
each affected layer clones and zeroes its intermediate tensor; the 0.28–0.43 ms
overhead is expected. The result shows the quality effect of the actual cumulative
mutation, but it must not be represented as physical speedup.

## Matched protocol

- Both arms start from the same SmolLM2-135M Q3 dataset, 409/52 train/validation
  partitions, 51 held-out candidates, fitted feature profile, seed, and ΔPPL <=
  0.01 safe label.
- One-shot fits once, ranks all 51 held-out safe probabilities, and selects the top
  10. Iterative selects one, reveals its already-measured single-channel outcome,
  moves that row into training, refits preprocessing and LightGBM, and repeats.
- The final sets each mask exactly 10 distinct layer/channel coordinates at once.
  This is a 17,280-coupled-parameter functional proxy (1,728 per selected channel)
  in either arm; no conclusion depends on summing the independent ΔPPL labels.
- The iterative path persists direct cumulative measurements after every step. Its
  final value and the one-shot value come from the same loaded checkpoint and
  calibration tokens.
- Latency uses one warmup and 21 measured 64-token forward passes per final arm.
  The report retains separate nearby baseline medians to expose system variance.
- The float16 Hugging Face mask path performs no encode/decode or requantization,
  so accumulated quantization error is exactly zero by construction in both arms.
  This is not evidence that a quantized iterative rewrite would have zero error.
- GPU hours include wall time spent in the cumulative GPU measurements; iterative
  additionally includes the recorded GPU evaluation time of each revealed label.
  Total study time also includes CPU LightGBM fitting.

## Validity boundary

This is one model, one final budget, and a 64-token calibration sample. The
iterative policy retrains on independently measured single-channel outcomes, not
on its cumulative path measurement, although every cumulative state is directly
evaluated. Masking preserves tensor shapes and storage, so the parameter number is
a functional compression proxy rather than a file-size reduction. Physical HF or
native GGUF removal is required to measure real latency/size gains, and quantized
rewrites are required to study accumulated codec error. The small quality advantage
does not establish that iterative pruning is generally worth its extra cost.
