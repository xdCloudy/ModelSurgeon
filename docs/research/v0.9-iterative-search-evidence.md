# v0.9 iterative surgery and repair evidence

Issue #188 was exercised as a two-generation, budget-two physical MLP-channel search on
`HuggingFaceTB/SmolLM2-135M` at immutable revision
`93efa2f097d58c2a74874c7e644dbc9b0cee75a2`. The candidate pool was original channels
0, 1, and 2. Every final arm removed two model-wide channels. Selection minimized the
backend's measured quality metric with a deterministic measurement-ID tie break.

The complete machine-readable record is
[`v0.9-iterative-search-study.json`](v0.9-iterative-search-study.json), SHA-256
`7be8a998995d8a7020c48232142761d8fe639c8b0c170c8c26c49e2563f7c0bc`.

## Runtime and protocol

- Host: Intel Core i5-14600K, NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3060.
- HF runtime: PyTorch 2.13.0+cu130, FP32 CUDA inference and repair, seven timed forward
  repetitions after two warmups.
- Native runtime: llama.cpp build 10567 at commit `d7fa69b7d`, six CPU threads, F16 GGUF,
  three llama-bench repetitions, and one content-addressed perplexity fixture chunk.
- Baseline GGUF: 270,885,184 bytes, SHA-256
  `a05340900ec91c645402a2a1073bf5e019af1c52403c748b295e951d97d52bf4`.
- Goals: quality increase at most 0.05, non-negative latency gain, and size gain of at
  least 0.01%.
- Repair: two bounded FP32 steps over only `model.norm.weight` (576 parameters). The
  native repair arm used the same selected topology, trained through the HF boundary,
  then exported to F16 GGUF for native deployment measurement.

The HF search selected channel 0 in generation one and channels (0, 2) in generation
two. Native GGUF selected channel 1 and then channels (1, 2). The different paths are
expected because HF loss and llama.cpp perplexity are distinct, independently measured
objectives.

## Final-arm comparison

| Backend | Arm | Quality change | Latency gain | Size gain | All goals |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| HF | no repair | -0.012316 loss | -1.09% | 0.0771% | no |
| HF | repair | -0.025459 loss | 3.86% | 0.0771% | yes |
| HF | one shot | -0.012316 loss | -1.09% | 0.0771% | no |
| native GGUF | no repair | +2.9422 PPL | 5.01% | 0.0765% | no |
| native GGUF | repair | +10.9825 PPL | 8.12% | 0.0765% | no |
| native GGUF | one shot | +2.9422 PPL | 5.01% | 0.0765% | no |

The matched one-shot and iterative native arms converged on the same pair and therefore
reuse one physical measurement rather than double-counting it. HF did the same. The
native result is deliberately reported as negative: the topology reduced size and
improved measured decode latency, but neither no-repair nor bounded repair retained
quality under the strict threshold. The repair itself was accepted by its HF validation
contract (validation loss change -0.013143), yet that improvement did not transfer to
the native perplexity fixture. This is evidence against treating repair acceptance on
one evaluator as proof of deployment-quality recovery.

## Cost and implementation findings

Unique HF measurements consumed 4.844 wall/GPU-seconds. Unique native measurements,
including physical execution, integrity validation, perplexity, and throughput, consumed
63.888 wall/CPU-seconds. Total accounted experiment cost was 68.732 wall-seconds, 4.844
GPU-seconds, and 63.888 CPU-seconds. Peak recorded HF allocation was 580,594,688 bytes;
peak native benchmark RSS was 724,848,640 bytes.

The real Windows run also exposed two runtime defects that synthetic fixtures did not:
the process-memory API needed explicit 64-bit ctypes signatures, and the F16 direct-copy
path recomputed invariant retained block runs for every row. Both were corrected and
covered by the existing focused suites before the successful rerun. The study runner
keeps native plan evidence compact because expanding a full model-wide identity remap is
not necessary to prove the executed family, layers, removed channels, tensor coupling,
or exact parameter/storage deltas.
