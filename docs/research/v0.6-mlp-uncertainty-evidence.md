for issue #107. It uses the same component-held-out validation split as the v0.5 proof and
does not consume held-out test labels.

## Validation comparison

| Method | Coverage | Calibration error | Top-10% error lift | Mean width | CPU seconds | Model bytes |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Deep ensemble | 0.0733 | 0.8267 | 1.1561× | 0.0419 | 42.859 | 133,865 |
| MC dropout | 0.1300 | 0.7700 | 1.5520× | 0.0886 | 20.109 | 26,773 |

MC dropout is selected because its coverage is less poor, and it also shows stronger
active-selection value at lower measured CPU and model-storage cost.

## Interpretation and negative result

Neither method produces a credible 90% interval: both are drastically under-dispersed.
The selected MC-dropout values must therefore be treated as ranking signals, not calibrated
confidence bounds. Its 1.55× top-decile error lift is nevertheless evidence that the signal
can prioritize informative candidates under a bounded evaluation budget.

This path remains optional. A disabled MLP uncertainty budget exits before evidence checks
or PyTorch use, and tree uncertainty workflows have no dependency on the MLP runner.
