# ModelSurgeon v1.0 scientific results and limitations

This release synthesis reports what the versioned evidence supports, what it does
not support, and where the evidence can be reproduced. It is deliberately bounded
to the persisted Q1–Q8, quantization, consumer-scale, and performance records. The
companion [machine-readable manifest](v1.0-scientific-results.json)
contains the same claim-to-evidence mapping and verifies each checked-in report's
content identity.

## How to read the evidence

The report uses content-addressed evidence run IDs such as
`run_v08_q3_5a3a1899a017a5c6`. Each ID is an immutable alias for the listed persisted
JSON report and its SHA-256; it is not presented as a new experiment execution.
The linked report contains the protocol, source revisions, seeds, measurements, and
reproduction command. Models, raw datasets, and generated weights remain external
artifacts and are not committed to this repository.

## Quantization and hardware

The quantized-feature study found RMS rank correlation of 0.9994 for Q4_K and 1.0
for Q5_K, Q6_K, and Q8_0 in the sampled tensors. Signed means had materially
higher relative error because their reference values are often near zero, and
quantized sparsity was not a reliable proxy. The matched loss study found that
requantization MSE decreased from Q4_K through Q8_0 and that the interaction with
the fixed surgery was small relative to the surgery term in the sampled regions.
These are weight-space and feature-quality results, not end-to-end quality claims.

The consumer scale study completed the seven-rung ladder from 134.5M to 7.25B on
one Windows 11 / RTX 3060 12 GB / 64 GiB host. The 4.02B rung fit the GPU in the
bounded FP16 protocol; the 7.25B rung used CUDA+CPU placement. These measurements
establish a host-specific feasibility envelope, not cross-hardware performance or
full-model cumulative-surgery behavior. The v1.0 performance guardrails passed 18
of 18 budgets, but their fixtures are regression sentinels rather than model-quality
or universal throughput benchmarks.

## Limitations and validity threats

### Prediction

- Most Q1–Q8 labels are reversible single-channel mask ΔPPL values on one short
  calibration corpus. They do not establish task-general or deployment-general
  predictive validity.
- Cross-model and cross-family results contain one or a small number of held-out
  targets. Ranking transfer is more supported than calibrated effect-size transfer.
- The Q7 result is model-specific and based on offline replay of already measured
  labels; it is not evidence of universal active-learning sample efficiency.

### Masking

- Functional masks preserve tensor shapes and storage. Their parameter counts are
  structural proxies, not file-size reductions.
- Q4's independent single-channel outcomes cannot be summed to predict a combined
  mutation. Q8 directly tested one cumulative path and remains a one-model study.
- Improved short-text perplexity is not a general quality improvement claim.

### Physical surgery

- Physical HF and native GGUF runs have separate evaluator and runtime identities.
  A mask result does not prove physical removal, latency improvement, or native
  deployment quality.
- The native iterative-search record reports a negative quality result despite
  size and latency gains; HF repair acceptance did not transfer to the native
  perplexity fixture. This is evidence against treating one evaluator as a proxy
  for another.

### Quantization

- Quantized feature reliability covers sampled MLP tensors and the project's
  reference codecs, not every tensor, codec implementation, or optimized runtime.
- The surgery/requantization decomposition is a weight-space study. It does not
  establish end-to-end perplexity, latency, or behavior after a full-model rewrite.
- Context features cannot be calibrated for unseen codecs or hardware without
  matched real outcomes; duplicated or counterfactually relabeled rows are excluded.

### Hardware and reproducibility

- Resource measurements are host-specific, often single-run feasibility checks, and
  must not be generalized to other GPUs, drivers, operating systems, or runtimes.
- Platform-specific smoke results must not be generalized to a different native
  runtime or operating system. The formal pinned GGUF compatibility matrix remains
  the authority for runtime support claims.
- Reproduction requires access to the pinned external model/data artifacts and the
  recorded tool/runtime revisions. Missing or inaccessible artifacts must be reported
  as unavailable, never replaced with fabricated values.

## Evidence integrity

The manifest is validated by `tests/test_scientific_results.py`: every referenced
JSON artifact must exist with the declared SHA-256, every claim must reference a
known evidence run ID, and the human report must expose the same IDs plus explicit
limitation categories. The manifest is the release index; the linked records remain
the authoritative detailed evidence.
